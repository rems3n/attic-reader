# Reference: "ancient-greek-resources-U-Chicago.pdf"

Source: file in the user's Drive folder (read in full). Despite the name
it is not a resource list: it is a bundle of first-year Attic Greek course
handouts keyed to Mastronarde, *Introduction to Attic Greek* (unit
numbers throughout), citing Smyth once. About 40 % of the Greek is
mojibake from a legacy font.

## Contents

1. Principal parts (present, future, aorist, gloss) for ~90 verbs grouped
   by Mastronarde unit 8–20; irregular ones shaded.
2. Breathings and accent rules with practice questions.
3. Conditional sentences: six types, protasis/apodosis mood table, μή in
   the protasis.
4. Subjunctive and optative uses, main and dependent ("may and might are
   for wimps"): hortatory, prohibitive, deliberative; wish, potential;
   purpose, fear, indirect deliberative questions, general/vivid
   conditionals, optative of secondary sequence.
5. Verb endings (primary/secondary/imperative), infinitive and participle
   shapes, accent behaviour.
6. Verb stem classes: vowel, labial, velar, dental, liquid; strengthened
   presents (-πτω, -ττω, -ζω, -λλω), nasal infixes, -σκ-, reduplication.
7. Paradigm tables: quantifiers, pronouns, 3rd declension including
   contracts, perfect system (οἶδα, δέδοικα, τέθνηκα, ἕστηκα), ἵστημι,
   εἰμί/εἶμι/φημί/οἶδα/εἶδον, -μι verbs, contract verbs, full λύω synopsis.

## Study advice found

- Learn stem types, not every verb ("Know λύω, and you'll know most vowel
  stems").
- Parsing: decide finite vs non-finite first; eliminate infinitives before
  participles.
- Identify a subjunctive/optative as independent vs dependent before
  assigning meaning.

## How the course uses it

- Grammar-note checklists for Stage 2: the six conditional types, the
  subjunctive/optative use list and the stem-class table map directly onto
  Units 8–10 and the `verb.*` / `syntax.cond.*` skills.
- Candidate gold cells for the morphology tests: δέδοικα / τέθνηκα /
  ἕστηκα short perfects, contract optatives, full ἵστημι (after decoding
  the font).
- Nothing on pronunciation beyond breathings; no resource assessments.

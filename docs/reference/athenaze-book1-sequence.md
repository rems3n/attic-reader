## Method (from the introduction)

- Inductive first: the story is read and understood before the grammar
  is presented; readings contain glossed grammar not yet taught and the
  teacher "should not dwell on them". Illustration captions are the first
  encounter with each chapter's new feature.
- Chapter shape: vocabulary list → α reading with glosses → Word Study
  (English derivatives) → grammar → exercises → cultural essay → β
  reading → Word Building (Greek word families) → grammar → exercises →
  tail reading with comprehension questions → English-to-Greek
  continuation sentences.
- Classroom: teacher reads aloud with phrasing, simple comprehension
  questions in English, sentence-by-sentence modelling and repetition,
  reread; questions in Greek suggested from Chapter 4.
- Vocabulary: no counts or frequency principle stated; important words
  are recycled constantly, some whole phrases recur "like Homeric
  formulae"; glossed words need not be learned; frequent quizzing both
  directions; obvious compounds not listed.
- Accents learned gradually; macrons printed throughout and to be written
  by the student.
- Pronunciation: refers to W. Sidney Allen and Stephen Daitz; vowel
  length is quantity not quality; -γγ- as -ng-; rough breathing on ρ.

## Exercise types (Book I)

Locate-in-reading (find all infinitives / middles / participles /
imperfects / relatives with antecedent); fill in article forms;
transformations (sg↔pl of whole sentences, person/number, active→middle,
present→aorist, imperfect and aorist side by side); parsing with reason;
paradigm writing; paired Greek→English / English→Greek sentences that
mirror word order; tail reading + comprehension questions + English→Greek
continuation of the story. Sample early English→Greek targets: "Dicaeopolis
is a farmer", "He always works in the field", "Why aren't you working?",
"Hurry, oxen; don't sleep in the field".

## How the course uses it

- Stage 1 (Units 1–6) in `COURSE_PLAN.md` follows this order with one
  deliberate change: the present middle and deponents come in Unit 3
  (Athenaze Ch. 6) and 3rd-declension consonant stems in Unit 3 (Ch. 7),
  matching Athenaze; participles are held to Unit 5 (Ch. 8–9); second
  aorist before first aorist (Ch. 11–12); imperfect after the aorists
  (Ch. 13). The plan's Units 4 and 5 were reordered accordingly (see the
  syllabus table).
- Adopt: caption-first introduction of the new feature on the cover
  image; "find the forms in the story" as an exercise type; the Word
  Study / Word Building pair (our `cognates.json` derivatives + a
  word-family drill); paired mirror-order sentences; continuation
  composition after the tail reading; macrons shown in vocabulary
  entries (the morphology engine already tracks hidden length).
- Our story is original (potter's family in Athens), so only the grammar
  order and the device set are borrowed.
